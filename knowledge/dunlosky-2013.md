## shows
A monograph that reviews 10 learning techniques students can use with little training. For each one, the authors assess whether its benefits hold across learning conditions, student characteristics, types of material and kinds of outcome measures. Practice testing and distributed practice received the highest utility ratings. Elaborative interrogation, self-explanation and interleaved practice received moderate ratings. Summarization, highlighting, the keyword mnemonic, imagery use for text learning and rereading received low ratings.
## limits
A review of earlier studies, not a test of any app, and the ratings are the authors' judgment of the evidence available in 2013. A low rating means the evidence for broad benefit was limited, not that the technique can never help. It does not say how long or how often a particular student should use a technique, or how well it works for one subject in one classroom.
